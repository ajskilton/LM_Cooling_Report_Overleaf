\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:Introduction}

The continued electrification of the global economy is driving a sustained
increase in electricity demand~\cite{international_energy_agency_electricity_2025},
and PECs enable this shift in transport~\cite{bosshard_multi-objective_2016} and
energy systems~\cite{steimer_enabled_2010}. Across all their applications, the
power density of PECs is increasing, driven by cost reduction, increased
functionality and, in automotive and aerospace applications, hard weight and
space limits~\cite{steimer_enabled_2010,biela_cooling_2007}. The trend has
persisted for decades, enabled largely by improvements in power-conversion
efficiency, and is projected to continue~\cite{morroni_understanding_2023}.

Inductive power transfer (IPT) systems, such as those used for electric vehicle
(EV) charging, transfer power across a large air gap between loosely coupled
coils. Many commercial designs already actively cool the coils, particularly on the
space- and weight-constrained vehicle side~\cite{lawton_power_2024}. However, Liu
\emph{et al.}\ recommend further research into new technologies for cooling
wireless chargers~\cite{liu_thermal_2025}.
