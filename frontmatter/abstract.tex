\textbf{\large ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

% Write your abstract here
As power electronic converters become more power-dense, removing their losses
from a smaller volume increasingly limits their design. Galinstan, a
room-temperature liquid metal with about 28 times the thermal conductivity of
water, can be driven by a magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) pump with no moving parts,
but its low volumetric heat capacity and high electrical conductivity bring
trade-offs that few studies have tested against water at converter scale. This
project evaluated MHD-pumped Galinstan as a coolant for high-power-density
converters and identified where in an inductive power transfer (IPT) system it
is most useful. A Galinstan loop and a water loop were compared on the same
commercial cold plate under resistive heat loads, the results were interpreted
with an $\varepsilon$--NTU thermal network and extended with a validated
conjugate heat transfer simulation, and the Galinstan loop was used to cool a
power-dense \SI{85}{\kilo\hertz} planar inductor.

At the flows each pump could deliver, water outperformed Galinstan, with a total
thermal resistance of \SIrange{0.257}{0.267}{\celsius\per\watt} against
\SIrange{0.488}{0.799}{\celsius\per\watt}. At
\SIrange{0.75}{1.45}{\milli\litre\per\second}, the Galinstan cold plate was
caloric-limited: its high conductivity made the convective resistance
negligible, and its low heat capacity set the performance. The MHD pump drew
\SI{0.85}{\watt}, against \SI{11.5}{\watt} for the water pump, but with a
mechanical efficiency of only \SI{0.56}{\percent}. The simulation shows that at
\SI{5}{\milli\litre\per\second} Galinstan would come within \SI{14}{\percent} of
water, and the model predicts that it would outperform water once the heated
area falls below about \SI{40}{\centi\metre\squared}. The Galinstan loop cooled
the inductor at more than twice the loss its core can dissipate without active
cooling, while the coolant added only \SI{1.0}{\milli\ohm} to the winding AC
resistance, as the cold plate shields it from the leakage field. Galinstan is
therefore best suited to small, high-flux components, such as the inverter,
rectifier and shielded compact magnetics of an IPT system, provided that more
efficient, higher-flow pumps are developed.
